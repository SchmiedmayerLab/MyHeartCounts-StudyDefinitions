# My Heart Counts Cardiovascular Health UK Study

## Participant Information Sheet

**Chief Investigator:** Professor Allan Lawrie, Imperial College London.

You are being invited to take part in a research study. Before you decide, it is important for you to understand why the research is being done and what it will involve. Please take time to read the following information carefully and discuss it with others if you wish.

Ask us if there is anything that is not clear or if you would like more information. Take time to decide whether or not you wish to take part.

Thank you for reading this.

Prof. Allan Lawrie

(Chief Investigator)

### Overview

You are invited to participate in a research study, called "My Heart Counts", which aims to improve understanding of heart health through the collection of health-related information and explore how digital tools may support engagement with health and physical activity. Your participation is completely voluntary, and you may withdraw at any time.

This study involves downloading and using an app “My Heart Counts” on your smartphone to collect health and activity information. The information collected through the app may be linked with information from your NHS healthcare records to help researchers better understand cardiovascular health. While you may not receive any direct benefits, your involvement will contribute to valuable research that could benefit many others by advancing our understanding of heart health.

### Do I have to take part?

It is up to you to decide whether to take part in this study and how long you wish to take part for. If you do decide to take part, you will be given a digital copy of this information sheet to keep and be asked to sign an e-consent form. If you decide to take part, you are still free to withdraw at any time without giving a reason. You may use the app as much or as little as you like, and you have options within the app to start or stop sharing different types of data or receive certain notifications. You can change these whenever you wish.

### What is the purpose of the study?

You are invited to participate in a research study using the My Heart Counts mobile application to collect information about physical activity, fitness, lifestyle and health. This study will combine information collected through the app with information from NHS healthcare records to help researchers better understand the relationships between activity, fitness, lifestyle behaviours and cardiovascular health. The information collected will also be used to develop and evaluate digital biomarkers and disease-classification models that may support future cardiovascular research.

Additionally, you have the option to participate in an eCoach component within the My Heart Counts to receive daily messages to promote physical activity. The purpose of this component is to evaluate whether personalised physical activity prompts generated by a Large Language Model (LLM) can increase engagement with physical activity. By checking your physical activity levels, engagement, and cardiovascular health metrics when you receive these prompts, the study aims to determine if personalised, AI-generated coaching can support participation in physical activity and healthy lifestyle behaviours.

### Why have I been invited?

We are inviting around 50,000 people in the UK to be involved in this study. We wish to include people with known cardiovascular conditions, as well as healthy volunteers.

### What will happen to me if I take part?

You will be asked to download the My Heart Counts app on your smartphone. The MHC app collects physical activity and health data that your phone health system already routinely collects, including any data collected through linked wearable devices (e.g. smart watch or ring). Once you have downloaded the app, you will go through screens that will determine eligibility, describe the study to you, ask you to provide informed consent, and ask if you would like to take part in the eCoach part of the study. If you provide consent, a copy of this consent form will be emailed to you.

**If you have been told by a medical professional that you cannot exercise for any reason, this study is not suitable for you.**

Once enrolled, you will be able to choose which categories of health and activity data you would like to share with the study team. You may update sharing preferences at any time. Subject to your permissions, the app may collect both historical information already stored within your phone health platform (up to 10 years prior) and new information generated during your participation in the study.

The My Heart Counts app includes a dashboard that displays summaries and visualisations of the health and activity information collected through the study, together with information about your participation, such as days enrolled and questionnaires completed.

**The MHC application is not a replacement for a healthcare provider. Information collected through the app and linked health records is not routinely reviewed in real time by the study team. The app should not be relied upon to identify health concerns, provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment or be used in an emergency. You should continue to contact your healthcare provider regarding your health and clinical care. Data from the app will NOT be shared with your doctor by the study team.**

If you choose to participate in the optional eCoach component, you will start receiving personalised daily activity messages after your first week in the study. You will receive one daily message for a period of 2 to 16 weeks or until you wish to stop. You may interact with these messages as much or as little as you wish and can stop receiving them without leaving the main study.

You can remain in the study for as long as you wish and may stop sharing data or withdraw from the study at any time.

### What information will be collected?

The study collects information from several sources.

During enrolment you will be asked to provide the following demographic information:

- Your email address

- Name

- Date of birth

- NHS number

- Height

- Weight

- Health conditions

- Race/Ethnicity

- Sex at birth (optional)

- Gender Identity (optional)

- Education level

- Household income

- Physical activity habits

- Preferred workout types

- Blood type (optional)

- Post code

If you are unhappy sharing any of the mandatory data fields, this study is not suitable for you.

With your permission, the app may collect health and activity data information routinely recorded by your phone or wearables health platform, including activity and exercise, body measurements, heart health, mobility, nutrition, exercise, respiratory health, sleep, symptoms, vital signs, hearing, mental wellbeing, and reproductive health. You may choose to give access to all or just part of the data, and you may update your data sharing preferences at any time

For some selected devices e.g. Apple Watch, the study may also collect sensor data such as motion information, environmental information, device usage reports, ECG measurements, optical heart-rate measurements, pedometer data, wrist temperature and information relating to frequently visited locations that does not include GPS coordinates or specific location names.

We will collect identifiable information about you, such as your NHS number (or CHI number in Scotland), full name, postcode and date of birth, so that information collected through the app can be linked with NHS healthcare records, disease registries, audit datasets and other approved research datasets where relevant to the study. Your NHS/CHI number and other necessary identifying information will be transferred securely to approved organisations carrying out the linkage process, such as Digital Health and Care Wales and the SAIL Databank. A de-identified version of the data collected through the MHC app will also be transferred to support the creation of the linked research dataset.

If you have chosen to opt out of your NHS data being used for research through the National Data Opt Out, we may not be able to link your NHS healthcare records to your study data. If you wish, you can review or change your choice at <https://www.nhs.uk/your-nhs-data-matters/manage-your-choice/>.

**Changing your National Data Opt-Out is entirely your choice. You do not need to change your preference in order to take part in the other parts of this study.**

We will also collect information about your device and how you use the MHC app. This may include device type, model and operating system, app version, dates and times app is used, app-opening frequency, interactions with notifications, technical information such as error or crashes. This information is collected automatically while you use the app and help us evaluate the performance of the app.

My Heart Counts app will NOT access your personal contacts, other applications, personal photos, texts, or email messages on your phone.

### What do I have to do?

You will be asked to:

- Download and register for the My Heart Counts app.

- Complete eligibility and consent procedures.

- Choose which health, activity and sensor data you wish to share

- Provide baseline demographic and health information

- Optionally, complete questionnaires about your health, lifestyle, physical activity, quality of life and app usage.

- Optionally, complete health-related tasks such as ECG recordings with your smartphone and/or wearable devices, 6-minute walk test, 12-minute run tests, where appropriate.

- Optionally, provide additional health information and measurements, such as blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol levels and other health-related metrics where available.

- Review information displayed within the app

- Optionally, receive and interact with the personalised eCoach messages.

**Most questionnaires take less than 5 minutes to complete, and active participation is expected to take no more than approximately 5-10 minutes per day.**

At the beginning of the study, you will be asked to complete a screening questionnaire to help determine whether participation in the active tasks is appropriate. Depending on your response, you may be advised to discuss participation with your doctor before undertaking any of the exercise related tasks. Participation in exercise-related tasks is optional, and you should only undertake activities that you feel comfortable completing.

If you agree to receive notifications, the app may remind you when questionnaires or tasks are due. Some questionnaires may be requested every two weeks, while other may be requested every 3 or 6 months. Certain questionnaires and activities are available to be completed whenever you choose.

As part of the study, you may be invited to participate in optional feedback activities, such as surveys, interviews or focus groups, to share your experiences of using the MHC app and, where applicable, the eCoach feature. These activities are intended to help us improve the platform and better understand participant experiences. Participation in these activities is entirely voluntary and choosing not to take part will not affect your participation in any other aspect of the study.

If you consent to future contact, you may also be contacted about additional research opportunities related to My Heart Counts.

### What are the possible disadvantages and risks of taking part?

The risks associated with taking part in this study are expected to be low.

Some optional physical activity tests such as the Six-Minute Walk Test, and the 12-Minute Run Test may pose some risks including cardiovascular events (e.g., increased heart rate and potential chest pain), musculoskeletal injuries (e.g., strains or sprains), dehydration, fatigue, and unanticipated medical events. Participation in these activities is optional, and you should only undertake activities that you feel comfortable completing.

As with any study involving the collection of personal and health information, there is a risk that the confidentiality or privacy of your information could be compromised. We use a range of technical, organisation and governance measures to protect your information and minimise these risks. Further details are provided in the section “HOW WILL WE USE INFORMATION ABOUT YOU”.

You may also receive notifications through the MHC app. Some participants may find these inconvenient or burdensome. You can choose whether to receive notifications and change your preferences at any time.

### What are the possible benefits of taking part?

We cannot guarantee that you will receive any benefits from this study. Participation in this study may help you become more informed about your fitness. Participation in the trial may help you increase your physical health.

### What will happen to my data?

All the data collected through the My Heart Counts app will be sorted in a secure platform hosted by Google Cloud within the UK. We will take steps to keep your information confidential, wherever possible, information that directly identifies you will be kept separately from your research data. Your research data will be given a unique study ID, meaning that researchers can usually work with your information without knowing who you are.

Data that are linked with your NHS healthcare records will be held securely within a Trusted Research Environment such as UK CliC, hosted within the SAIL Databank, Swansea University. The linked research dataset will use a pseudonymised identifier rather than your name or NHS/CHI number, so researchers will not routinely have access to information that directly identifies you.

Only authorised individuals will be able to access pseudonymised study data, and access will be limited according to their role in the study.

### Who has access to my data?

Authorised researchers from the study team will have access to de-identified datasets and will not routinely have access to the information that directly identifies you.

Researchers form partner universities, such as Stanford University, and other research collaborators may also access pseudonymised or anonymised study data where this is necessary to carry out the research.

Approved researchers outside the study team from academic, public and commercial organisations may also be given access to anonymised linked data within the Trusted Research Environment for approved research purposes. They will not have access to information that directly identifies you.

### Can I withdraw from the study?

You can withdraw from the study at any moment without giving a reason and no further data will be collected from you. If you withdraw from the study, data collected before your withdrawal will normally continue to be retained and used for the purposes of our research.

If you wish to have your data deleted, you should contact the study team via email (myheartcountsuk@imperial.ac.uk). We will be able to delete your data where possible, unless it has already been included in research analysis or where required to retain for legal or regulatory reasons. Data that has already been transferred for linkage with your health records cannot deleted, but it will not continue to be linked to NHS records.

If you lose the ability to make decisions about taking part in the study during your participation, no new information will be actively collected from you through the app. Information collected before you lost capacity may continue to be retained and used for the purposes of the research, in accordance with applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

### What if something goes wrong?

If you are harmed by taking part in this research project, there are no special compensation arrangements. If you are harmed due to someone’s negligence, then you may have grounds for a legal action. Regardless of this, if you wish to complain or have any concerns about any aspect of the way you have been treated during the course of this study then you should immediately inform the Investigator (Prof. Allan Lawrie at <myheartcountsuk@imperial.ac.uk>). The normal National Health Service complaints mechanisms are also available to you. If you are still not satisfied with the response, you may contact the Imperial College Research Governance Integrity Team (<rgitcoordinator@imperial.ac.uk>).

### How will we use information about you after the study ends?

Once we have finished the study, we will keep some of the data so we can check the results. We will write our reports in a way that no-one can work out that you took part in the study.

We will keep your study data for a maximum of 10 years after the study has closed. We will keep the study open for as long as we have funding to do so, in the first instance we expect this will be 5 years. The study data will then be fully anonymised and securely stored.

### What will happen to the results of the research study?

Outputs from the study will be presented at conferences, published in scientific literature and on our website <https://www.imperial.ac.uk/nhli/research/my-heart-counts/>. We will also share updates via the app.

The results of this research study may be presented at scientific or medical meetings or published in scientific journals. Your identity and/or your personal health information will not be disclosed except as authorized by you or as required by law. However, there is always some risk that even de-identified information might be re-identified.
